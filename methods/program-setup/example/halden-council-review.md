# Review: Halden Software Governance Council draft charter

I checked `council-draft.md` against the ten program-setup steps, the launch checklist and SOP, and the charter template. The draft handles intake and success measures well. It has no sponsor, 14 voting members, no written decision rights or thresholds, no procurement enforcement, and meetings with no agenda or decision log. As written, the council would have nothing to enforce its decisions and would make them slowly.

## Gap table

| Step | What's missing | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Purpose [XW-003] | The purpose says the council oversees software and applications. It doesn't say what goes wrong today. | Without a stated problem, nobody can tell later whether the council is working. Oversight bodies with no problem to solve tend to become rubber stamps. | Write one sentence naming the current failure, such as duplicate purchases, renewals nobody tracks, or unreviewed AI tools. **Decision needed:** which problem the sponsor wants fixed. |
| 2. Sponsor [XW-027] | No sponsor section, and no executive who controls the software budget. The CIO is a voting member but isn't named as sponsor. | No sponsor means no enforcement. Nobody signs the charter, and the council's decisions have no budget behind them. | Add a Sponsor section with name, title and the budget they control. **Decision needed:** who sponsors. The CIO and CFO are the obvious candidates. |
| 3. Decision rights [XW-027] | Scope lists new requests, renewals, retirements and AI tools. It doesn't say which of these the council decides and which it only advises on. The cost or risk threshold is mentioned but never defined, and there's no AI-use threshold. No role is named to decide items below threshold. | Undefined thresholds mean the triage analyst decides case by case what reaches the council. That leaves two outcomes: everything goes up and the council becomes a bottleneck, or nothing does and it is bypassed. Items below threshold also have no owner. | Fill in the template's decision-rights table: each decision gets one accountable party, a threshold, and a named delegated role for items below it. **Decision needed:** the cost figure, the risk tier and the AI-use criteria that send an item to the council. |
| 4. Membership [XW-004] | 14 voting members: CIO, CISO, 11 function heads (procurement, finance, legal, architecture, HR, operations, warehousing, transport, customer service, sales, marketing) and the service desk lead. No chair is named. The service desk lead both votes and takes the minutes. | Anything over 9 voting members slows decisions and makes a quorum hard to reach. The secretary should be a neutral role, not a voter. And without a chair, nobody owns the agenda or breaks ties first. | Cut to 5–9 voters: CIO, CISO, and the heads of procurement, finance, legal and architecture, plus one or two business leads. Make the rest consulted and non-voting. Move minute-taking to a non-voting secretary; the governance analyst is the natural choice since they already triage. Name a chair. **Decision needed:** which business leads vote and who chairs. |
| 5. Intake [XW-027] | Nothing substantial. There's one form, a named triage role (the governance analyst) and weekly triage, and only above-threshold items go to the meeting. | Partial: the filter only works once step 3's thresholds are written down. | Add exceptions as an explicit route to the meeting. No separate intake gap. |
| 6. Enforcement [XW-027] | Procurement isn't told to refuse unapproved above-threshold purchases. Overlaps with other boards (architecture review, change advisory, security) aren't settled. | Without the procurement block, the intake form is optional. Unsettled overlaps mean an item can be reviewed twice, or by nobody. | Add the template's Enforcement section with the procurement rule and a routing table for adjacent bodies. **Decision needed:** whether an architecture or change board exists and what it owns. The draft doesn't say. |
| 7. Cadence [XW-025] | The draft sets monthly 90-minute meetings with minutes. It has no standing agenda, no decision log (minutes don't record decisions as structured entries), and no out-of-cycle path for urgent items. | Urgent requests will either wait up to a month or bypass the council. Without a decision log, the council can't show an auditor or a new sponsor what it decided and why. | Add a standing agenda, a decision log with an owner and a published location, and a path for urgent items (for example, chair plus two voting members by email, ratified at the next meeting). Seed the first meeting with a live item. |
| 8. Escalation [XW-027] | Nothing substantial. Disputes go to the VP of IT Operations, who breaks ties. | The step is met on paper, but there's one concern. The CIO votes on the council, and a VP of IT Operations normally reports to the CIO and doesn't control the finance, legal or business budgets. Such a VP would struggle to settle a dispute between, say, the CIO and the head of finance. **Unverified:** the reporting line. Halden's org chart isn't in the input. | The SOP puts the chair first and the sponsor as the final tie-break. **Decision needed:** keep the VP of IT Operations, or escalate to the sponsor once one is named. I haven't counted this as a charter gap. |
| 9. Success measures [XW-025] | Nothing substantial. All four measures are there, reviewed quarterly, with a baseline set in the first quarter. | Met. It lines up with the day-90 metrics review [XW-025]. | Optional: add targets once the baseline exists. |
| 10. Launch plan and approval [XW-020] | No 90-day plan, no annual charter review date, no sponsor approval line. | Without these, nothing forces the council to launch, get approved or be reviewed. The SOP also requires keeping the sponsor's approval as evidence. | Add a review date and an approval line. Add a plan: charter approved and members named (days 0–30), intake and decision log live (by day 30), three monthly meetings (days 30–90), first metrics review (day 90). **Decision needed:** sponsor sign-off. |
