Application management program self-assessment

Download Word (.docx) Download Excel (.xlsx)

About 6 minutes to read (an estimate at 200 words per minute).

What you tick or type stays in this browser. The Word and Excel downloads are blank copies. To keep your answers, use Download my answers (Word), or print this page or save it as PDF.

A self-assessment for an IT application management program: the work of deciding what applications the organization has, who is accountable for each, what to buy, renew, change, or retire, and how to prove it. It records two states for each part of the program: where you are now (the As-Is state) and where you need to be (the To-Be state).

Use it before the full itam-maturity check, or alongside Tier self-assessment. The tier check tests your asset data against a standard's tiers, and the maturity check scores nine asset management areas with evidence. This one looks at the application management program as a whole and where you want it to go. Record your answers in Application management gap plan template.

Clause references are to the shared crosswalk (XW- rows). Nothing here comes from a standard's text, and the scale is this kit's own.

The scale

Score each dimension from 0 to 3. Use the evidence rule: if you cannot show the evidence for a level, score one level lower.

The scale
LevelMeaningWhat you can show
0Not doneNothing: nobody does this.
1Ad hocIt happens sometimes, depends on who is asked, and is not written down.
2DefinedIt is written down, has a named owner, and is followed. You can show a recent example.
3MeasuredIt is defined and followed, a number tells you how well it works, and the number has led to a change.

Not every dimension needs to reach 3. Level 2 is a strong result for a small organization. Choose each To-Be level for a reason: a risk you must reduce, a cost you want to save, or a requirement you must meet.

How to run it

  1. Agree scope: which applications, business units, and teams the program covers. Write it down.
  2. Score As-Is for each dimension with the people who do the work. For each score, write the evidence you could show an auditor or a leader. Apply the evidence rule.
  3. Choose To-Be for each dimension. Write why you chose it and by when. Leave a dimension where it is if it is not worth the effort.
  4. Work out the gap: To-Be minus As-Is. Rank the gaps by the risk or cost of leaving them. A gap of two or more is a candidate for a first-quarter project; a gap of one may fit into routine work.
  5. Name an owner and a first action for each gap you will close. Limit yourself to three at a time. Decision needed: the sponsor chooses which gaps to fund first.
  6. Record everything in Application management gap plan template. Repeat the assessment at least yearly, and after a merger, a platform change, or a change of sponsor. Keep each year's copy so you can show movement.

Common mistakes

  • Scoring by intent. A written policy nobody follows is level 1, not level 2.
  • Setting every To-Be to 3. Set targets you can afford to reach.
  • Scoring without the people who do the work. A sponsor's view of the program often differs from the practitioner's.
  • Skipping the evidence column. Without evidence the score cannot be challenged later.
  • Treating the assessment as the end. Its output is a short plan with owners and dates.
