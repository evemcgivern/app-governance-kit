IT security

Typical starting point
A current list of what is installed and where, so unmanaged or vulnerable items can be found.

Service desk

Typical starting point
Who has what, and its status, so they can support users and handle requests.

Procurement

Typical starting point
What is already owned, what renews soon, and which contract terms apply, before they buy.

Finance

Typical starting point
Cost by application and owner, renewal dates, and savings that have been confirmed.

Legal and compliance

Typical starting point
License terms, proof of purchase, and readiness for an audit.

HR

Typical starting point
Triggers for joiners, movers, and leavers, so access and licenses follow people.

Application owners

Typical starting point
Cost, usage, renewal dates, and the decisions they must make and when.

IT operations and change

Typical starting point
Configuration and ownership data that stays in step with changes.

Executives and sponsor

Typical starting point
A few measures that link to money saved or risk reduced, and the decisions they must make.

Internal audit and risk

Typical starting point
Evidence that controls run, and a way to sample records.

Publishers and external auditors (through counsel)

Typical starting point
Only what counsel releases, in the form counsel approves.

Notes

One row per customer of asset management information: the people and teams whose decisions depend on your data. Pre-filled with eleven common customers and a typical starting point for each; confirm or change it with them.

  • what_they_need_from_itam, level_of_detail, how_often: what they need, in how much detail, and how often. Ask them; do not guess.
  • what_we_need_from_them: what you need back, such as leaver triggers from HR or contract terms from Legal.
  • how_we_deliver and measure_of_success: how they get it, and how you would know you served them well.
  • owner and last_confirmed_on: who looks after this customer, and when you last checked the need is still right.

Use it in the first month, and again when a new team starts to rely on your data. It tells you what to collect and what you can stop collecting.
