What good looks like

A worksheet for deciding how far your program needs to go, so you aim for what is right for your organization and not for every standard at once. Full conformance to a standard is optional. Choose it only when a customer, a regulator, or a contract asks for it. The standard's tiers are informative, not required.

Use it before you set the To-Be levels in the assessment. It takes about 20 minutes. Clause references are to the shared crosswalk (XW- rows).

Step 1: Answer eight questions

Answer yes or no for your organization today. The last two columns tell you what the answer suggests.

Step 2: Read the answers

  • Start with a target of level 2 for every dimension you will work on this year. Level 2 means written down, owned, followed, with a recent example. It is a strong result.
  • For each yes above, consider level 3 only on the dimensions it names, and only where a number could prove it works.
  • If the sponsor answer is no, stop. Get a sponsor first. Targets without one do not get funded.
  • This is a rule of thumb, not a standard. Write down why you chose each target.

Step 4: Choose a few measures

Pick up to five measures. Prefer ones that cannot be met by paperwork alone. The Ideas sheet in application-management-workbook.xlsx lists credible ones. Record each in the Measures sheet or Benefits tracker template, with a baseline and a date. XW-025

Step 5: Say what you will not do yet

Naming what is out of scope protects the program from drifting. List up to three things you are deliberately leaving for later, and when you will look again.
