Rolling internal check

Download Word (.docx) Download Excel (.xlsx)

What you tick or type stays in this browser. The Word and Excel downloads are blank copies. To keep your answers, use Download my answers (Word), or print this page or save it as PDF.

A small, regular test of how your program really works, so an external audit holds no surprises. Instead of one large audit a year, test two or three areas each quarter, fix what you find, and test again. The yearly internal audit and management review in Internal audit and management review checklist then becomes a summary of work already done.

It is a check on the program, not on people. Findings are for fixing. Clause references are to the shared crosswalk (XW- rows).

The five steps

  1. Choose this quarter's areas. Pick two or three from the table below. Start with the one you worry about most. XW-025
  2. Take a sample. Pull the records the test needs, not the ones you already know are good. Write the sample size down.
  3. Test each record against the rule. A record either passes or fails. Do not average.
  4. Log every failure in Internal check log template, with a severity, an owner, and a date. XW-020
  5. Fix, then test again next quarter. A finding is closed only when a retest passes.

The sample sizes are suggestions. Use a larger sample for a risky area.

A four-quarter rotation

A simple plan so every area is tested at least once a year. Change it to fit your risks.

FirstRetirement, renewals

FourthInventory accuracy, plus the areas that failed earlier

Keep it useful

  • Test what you can fix. A finding nobody can act on only adds noise.
  • Report the trend, not only the count. Fewer repeat findings is the sign of a healthy program.
  • Share the log with your sponsor each quarter. Use it in the leadership report.
  • Before an external audit, run the same tests on the products the publisher named.
